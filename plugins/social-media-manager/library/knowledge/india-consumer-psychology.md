# India consumer psychology for short video

Used by `concept`, `hooks`, and later `script` and `localise`. It covers small businesses selling to Indian audiences, especially in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities (Indore, Jabalpur, the rest of MP and similar).

Sections: 1 how to use · 2 what the evidence says · 3 insight starters · 4 clichés to retire · 5 lever catalogue (L01–L20) · 6 hypotheses to test · 7 audience variants · 8 language · 9 sources.

---

## 1. How to use this file

- **Levers are tools, not tricks.** Pick **1–2 levers per concept**: the ones the offer can actually back up. Five levers in a 20-second reel means none of them land.
- **Every lever needs proof.** A real price, a real place, a real customer, a real process, a real date. If the proof doesn't exist yet, write `{{needed: …}}` and ask for it. Never fill the gap with an invented number, review or customer.
- **Start from the insight, then choose the lever.** The lever amplifies a truth (§3); it can't replace one.
- **Facts vs hypotheses.** §2 is research-backed (with sample limits). §6 is common belief that we test per client. Never present a hypothesis as fact in a pitch to the client.
- **Learnings beat this file.** If a client's `learnings` say a lever or formula worked or flopped for them, that wins over anything general here.

## 2. What the evidence says

Each truth: the finding (paraphrased), what it means for a reel, the source and a re-check date. Rukam samples are **SEC A, online, 18–60**: affluent, connected non-metro buyers, not all of Bharat. Hakuhodo's India sample is **three metros only**. So for Tier 3 mass-market and rural audiences, treat these as **directional**.

### T1. Proof beats hype: they research, and they want to see it working
- **Finding:** Non-metro buyers research before buying and want demonstrations and real use more than discounts or brand recall. YouTube reviews are the main discovery route for 37 %; 35 % of Tier 2–3 buyers check e-commerce sites first; 43 % of Tier 3 buyers check the brand's own website.
- **For a reel:** show the thing working on real footage: the process, the result, the counter, the van, the site. Seeing it beats claiming it. Specific details (a time, a price, a step) signal "this is real".
- **Source:** Rukam Capital, *Beyond Metros* (Jan 2026, preview). **Re-check by 2027-01-16.**

### T2. Real people and creators beat celebrities
- **Finding:** About 22–23 % trust creator recommendations; about 3 % are swayed by celebrities.
- **For a reel:** put the owner, staff or a real (consenting) customer on camera; use creator grammar (POV, talking head, "main aapko dikhata hoon") over polished ad grammar. A local face is an asset; a lookalike model is a cost.
- **Source:** Rukam Capital, *Beyond Metros* (Jan 2026). **Re-check by 2027-01-16.**

### T3. Trust travels by word of mouth, and service is part of the product
- **Finding:** Word of mouth sways 22–25 % (figures differ across the preview's pages); about 32 % of Tier 2–3 buyers weigh how customer service treats them **before** buying.
- **For a reel:** show how you treat people (the call back, the careful packing, the owner who answers). Local social proof ("Vijay Nagar mein…") works when it's true and specific.
- **Source:** Rukam Capital, *Beyond Metros* (Jan 2026). **Re-check by 2027-01-16.**

### T4. WhatsApp is the action channel
- **Finding:** About 84–90 % use WhatsApp, which the report calls the backbone for talking, discovering and buying in Tier 2 and 3.
- **For a reel:** the default CTA is WhatsApp ("WhatsApp karo"), shown on screen and said once. Design payoffs people forward to a family or society group: a price card, a checklist, a before/after.
- **Source:** Rukam Capital, *Beyond Metros* (Jan 2026). **Re-check by 2027-01-16.**

### T5. Value-conscious, deal-aware buyers
- **Finding:** 52 % of Tier 2 buyers track sale days; discounts are still the top motivator, and 47–48 % research deals or wait for them before buying.
- **For a reel:** state the offer plainly with the real price and the real end date. Frame value (what you get, what you save, what it costs per day) instead of "cheap", which many owners dislike.
- **Sources:** Rukam Capital, *Beyond Metros* (Jan 2026); Rukam Capital × YouGov, *Aspirations of New India* (22 Sep 2025, via coverage). **Re-check by 2027-01-16; *Aspirations* was due 2026-09-22 (overdue: look for a 2026 edition).**

### T6. Festivals and occasions trigger buying, especially beyond the metros
- **Finding:** In Tier 3, festivals are stronger buying triggers than sale calendars (no figure given). At festival time, 61 % of Tier 1 buyers lean towards homegrown brands, and about a third prefer products with cultural value.
- **For a reel:** tie the offer to the occasion's **real job** (clean the curtains before guests arrive at Diwali; get sarees ready before the wedding season; book the site visit during Navratri's auspicious days). Plan these reels ahead.
- **Sources:** *Beyond Metros* (Jan 2026); *Aspirations of New India* (Sep 2025, coverage). **Re-check as above.**

### T7. Local and small businesses start with an advantage
- **Finding:** 58 % prefer buying from local or small businesses, because they are easy to reach, have founder stories people relate to, and offer real value. Shoppers link local businesses with trust.
- **For a reel:** lean into being local: area names, the owner's own story, "apne shehar ka". Don't imitate national brands; the human scale is the advantage.
- **Source:** *Aspirations of New India* (Sep 2025, via MediaNews4U, Storyboard18, BestMediaInfo). **Overdue for re-check (2026-09-22).**

### T8. Authentic and useful beats glossy and aspirational
- **Finding:** 76 % value honest, authentic communication from brands, especially local ones. In *Beyond Metros*, usefulness beats hype: status cues move 13 %, nostalgia 12 %, pop-culture references 4 %.
- **For a reel:** plain talk, the real place, a practical answer to an everyday problem. Aspiration works when it's grounded (a nicer home for Diwali), not when it's borrowed luxury.
- **Sources:** *Aspirations of New India* (Sep 2025, coverage); *Beyond Metros* (Jan 2026). **Re-check as above.**

### T9. Friendship sits alongside family, and the clichés have aged
- **Finding:** 42 % of urban Indians call relationships at home a major stress (the highest of 9 Asian markets); 61 % find satisfaction in dependable friendships; 49 % ask friends about big decisions. Relationships are taking on a friendship style, including parent–child and couples. Hakuhodo India's COO asked marketers to show this honestly **without clichés**; shallow "buddy" portrayals trivialise it; hyperlocal detail belongs in the execution, not the core idea.
- **For a reel:** friends as advisers ("dost ne bataya"), siblings and couples as teammates, parents as friends. Avoid the tired templates in §4.
- **Source:** Hakuhodo India, *Emerging Aspirations: Hakuhodo India Trends 2025* (Sep 2025; Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore only; via Agency Reporter and Campaign India). **Overdue for re-check (2026-09-10).** Tier 2/3 use is a hypothesis (§6, HY8).

## 3. Insight starters: tensions worth looking for

An insight is one sentence: **"[audience] wants [X], but [Y] stands in the way, because [Z]."** It should make a customer say "haan, bilkul yahi hota hai." Look for a tension:
- **Human:** what a person wants vs what stops them.
- **Category:** what the category promises vs what it delivers.
- **Cultural:** what people say vs what they do.

These are **starting points to check against the brief and research**, not insights by themselves:

| Tension | Typical shape | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|---|
| Trust vs convenience | They'd love the service, but they don't hand over something precious to a shop they can't see inside. | Saree dry clean (T1, T3) |
| Time-poor vs house pride | Working couples want a proper home for guests, but there are no free hours before the festival. | Diwali deep cleaning (T6) |
| Value vs quality fear | They hunt for the deal, but "sasta" makes them suspect the quality. | Offer reels (T5) |
| Family decision vs personal choice | The buyer decides, but parents or the spouse must agree, and someone has to convince them. | Real estate, coaching (T9) |
| Local loyalty vs national polish | They prefer the local shop, but the national brand *looks* more professional online. | Clothing, food (T7) |
| "Log kya kahenge" vs real need | They need it (skin, weight, money help), but asking feels embarrassing. | Clinic, salon, finance: handle with care, no shaming |
| Jugaad pride vs paying for service | They can manage it themselves, but the do-it-yourself version costs a weekend or ruins the thing. | Home services |

Deeper digging when the brief is thin: what do customers say on WhatsApp or the phone (`customer_words`, research `customer_phrases`)? What do they do just before they call? What did they try first?

## 4. Clichés to retire

Category-default images that make a reel look like every other one. Use one only if the concept **flips** it (and say how).
- Joint family round the dining table, everyone laughing.
- Dadi's nuskha / mummy-knows-best as the only source of wisdom.
- Diya-and-rangoli montage with a generic festive track, product at the end.
- The shy bride, the judging aunty, the nosy neighbour as the villain.
- Stock "happy Indian family" in a flat that doesn't look like the city.
- Gully cricket, chai-tapri bonding, "bro" banter, used as decoration.
- "No.1 in Indore", "best quality", "trusted by thousands" with nothing behind it.

Research's `competitors_overdo` and `visual_cliches` for the client's niche add to this list per job.

## 5. Lever catalogue

Format per lever: **what it is** · **fits when** · **Hinglish example** (fictional; replace `{{…}}` with real facts) · **misuse**. The misuse line is the hard limit: if a concept needs the misuse to work, drop the lever.

**L01 Local social proof.** People like them, nearby, already chose this. · Fits: services and shops with real local customers. · "Vijay Nagar ke {{N}} ghar ab pickup se kapde dhulwate hain." · Misuse: invented counts or reviews; "trusted by thousands" with no basis.

**L02 Authority and expertise.** Years, skill or credentials that make the advice believable. · Fits: services where judgement matters (clinic, coaching, real estate, tailoring). · "{{N}} saal se Napier Town mein ghar bana rahe hain. Ek cheez sab bhool jaate hain." · Misuse: made-up years or titles; health or finance advice without real qualifications.

**L03 Risk reversal.** Take the fear of a bad outcome away: guarantee, free trial, pay after, free re-do. · Fits: first-time buyers, high-trust services. · "Pasand nahi aaya? Dobara press, free." (only if the client offers it) · Misuse: a guarantee the client hasn't confirmed, or one hidden behind conditions.

**L04 Before/after contrast.** Show the change; the gap does the persuading. · Fits: cleaning, salon, interiors, tailoring, fitness. · "Wahi sofa. Bas Diwali se pehle dhulwaya." · Misuse: edited or AI-exaggerated "after"; a before shot that shames someone's body or home.

**L05 Specificity.** Concrete details (a time, a step, a place, a real number) read as true. · Fits: almost always, as a booster to another lever. · "Subah 10 baje pickup, shaam 6 baje delivery." · Misuse: precise-looking numbers that nobody measured.

**L06 Value framing and charm pricing.** Make the price feel fair: per-day cost, what's included, a price below a round number. · Fits: offers, menus, packages. · "Sirf ₹299 se upar pickup free." / "Din ka ₹{{X}}." · Misuse: a fake "original" price to anchor against; calling it "cheap" if the brand dislikes the word.

**L07 Loss aversion (honest).** Show what they're really losing by waiting: time, money, a ruined thing. · Fits: maintenance, seasonal services, real deadlines. · "Har saal shaadi se pehle saree ki chamak thodi kam hoti jaati hai." · Misuse: fear-mongering, health scares, "aapka ghar kharab ho jayega".

**L08 Reciprocity.** Give something useful first: a tip, a checklist, a free check. · Fits: teach reels, expert businesses, cold audiences. · "Dry clean dene se pehle yeh 3 sawaal pucho, chahe kahin bhi do." · Misuse: a "free tip" that is really a disguised pitch.

**L09 Occasion and festival timing.** Attach the offer to the occasion's real job and timing. · Fits: Diwali, wedding season, Navratri, Eid, Rakhi, back-to-school, monsoon. · "Mehmaan aane se pehle curtain fresh. 25 Nov tak 20% off." · Misuse: trivialising rituals; a deadline that isn't real.

**L10 Family and friend benefit.** The purchase helps someone they care about. · Fits: real estate, education, health, gifting. · "Maa-papa ke liye ground floor, lift ki tension khatam." · Misuse: guilt-tripping ("achhe bete hote toh…"); clichéd family tableaux (§4).

**L11 Convenience and time saved.** Remove effort, travel or waiting. · Fits: pickup/delivery, home services, booking. · "Ghar baithe WhatsApp karo, hum le jaayenge." · Misuse: promising timings the business can't keep.

**L12 Grounded aspiration.** A believable step up in their own life, not borrowed luxury. · Fits: real estate, clothing, coaching, salons. · "Apna ghar, apne sheher mein. Rent ka hisaab band." · Misuse: lifestyle the product doesn't deliver; status shaming.

**L13 Humour and relatability.** A shared everyday moment, played lightly. · Fits: brands with a friendly voice; awareness reels. · "Press wala phir nahi aaya. Office ki meeting 10 baje." · Misuse: jokes at someone's body, caste, religion, region or gender; cringe bait.

**L14 Curiosity gap.** Name the kind of answer, hold the answer back, pay it off fast. · Fits: hooks for teach and proof reels. · "Saree dry clean mein ek step hai jo zyada log skip karte hain." · Misuse: a gap the video never closes; "you won't believe" bait.

**L15 Show the process (transparency).** Let them see inside: the kitchen, the workshop, the site. · Fits: trust-heavy services (T1). · "Aapki saree counter pe aate hi pehle yeh hota hai." · Misuse: staged or AI-generated "process" shown as the real place.

**L16 Real-person voice.** The owner, staff or a consenting customer speaks in their own words. · Fits: local businesses (T2, T7). · "Main Neha, yeh meri dukaan hai, aur yeh sawaal mujhe roz aata hai." · Misuse: AI or hired actors presented as real customers; undisclosed paid endorsements.

**L17 Local pride and belonging.** "One of us": the city, the area, the community the business serves. · Fits: shops with deep local roots. · "Indore ka apna dry cleaner, Vijay Nagar se." · Misuse: putting down other cities or communities.

**L18 Honest admission.** Admit a small, real limit; it makes the rest believable. · Fits: premium or slower services. · "Hum sabse sasta nahi hain. Par saree wapas waise hi milegi." · Misuse: fake modesty that is really a boast.

**L19 Immediate benefit.** What they get today, not someday. · Fits: offers, services with a fast result. · "Aaj book karo, kal subah pickup." · Misuse: overstating speed.

**L20 One clear choice.** One offer, one action; no menu of options in a 20-second reel. · Fits: every CTA. · "Bas WhatsApp karo: 'CURTAIN'." · Misuse: hiding conditions to keep it "simple".

**Pairings that work:** L15 + L05 (show it, with specifics) · L09 + L06 (occasion + fair price) · L16 + L03 (real person + risk taken away) · L04 + L11 (result + no effort) · L08 + L02 (free tip + earned authority).

## 6. Hypotheses to test (not facts)

Common beliefs with no strong public evidence for our audiences. Use them, but label them `hypothesis` in concepts, and let the learning loop decide per client (`learnings` with `hook_formula_id` / `angle_type` / `outcome`).

| Id | Hypothesis | How we'd see it |
|---|---|---|
| HY1 | "Paisa vasool" value framing beats a bare % discount for Tier 2/3 buyers. | Compare two offer reels for the same client. |
| HY2 | City or area pride ("Indore ka apna") lifts local shares. | Shares and saves vs the client's median. |
| HY3 | For Tier 3 and 35+ audiences, Devanagari on-screen text beats Roman Hinglish. | Same reel, two text versions (story or ad test). |
| HY4 | Owner on camera beats a voice-over for trust in Tier 2/3. | Enquiries per 1,000 views. |
| HY5 | Below about ₹1,000, a rupee amount ("₹200 bachao") beats a percentage; above it, the percentage reads bigger (adapted from the Rule of 100). | Offer reel A/B. |
| HY6 | "Family group mein bhejo" earns more sends than "follow karo". | Sends per reach. |
| HY7 | Festival reels posted 7–10 days ahead beat ones posted on the day. | Enquiries before the festival. |
| HY8 | Friend-group framing beats family framing for under-30s in Tier 2 cities (T9 is metro-only). | Saves and comments. |
| HY9 | A visible price on the first frame lifts enquiries for offer reels. | Already a `watch` learning for one sample client. |

When a hypothesis is confirmed for a client, it becomes a `learnings` entry (`outcome: worked`), not an edit to this file. Edit §2 only when new published research appears.

## 7. Audience variants

| | Metro (Tier 1) | Tier 2 (Indore, Jabalpur, Bhopal) | Tier 3 and small towns | Rural / vernacular-first |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Language** | English or English-led Hinglish; Hinglish for mass brands | Roman Hinglish (default) | Hindi-leaning Hinglish; Devanagari text is an option | Hindi (Devanagari); regional languages written natively, never translated |
| **Proof that works** | Reviews, specifics, sleek demo | Real place + owner + local proof | Seeing it work, the owner's face, the brand's own site/number (T1) | Demonstration, a known local face, word of mouth |
| **CTA** | DM, link, website | WhatsApp, call, visit | WhatsApp, call, visit the shop | Call, visit, WhatsApp voice note |
| **Pace and style** | Fast cuts, wit | Clear, warm, one joke at most | Slower, fully clear, nothing left implied | Slow, spoken, very visual |
| **Watch-outs** | Over-explaining | Copying metro slang | Clever wordplay, English jargon | Text-heavy screens |

**Younger (18–30):** creator style, friend-group settings (T9), humour, speed. **Older (35+):** clarity, respect, larger text, Hindi-leaning, the owner's credibility, family benefit (L10) without clichés.

## 8. Language

- **Roman Hinglish by default** for Instagram and reels. **Devanagari** for older, Tier 3, vernacular-first audiences, mass WhatsApp broadcasts and Hindi-first platforms. **English** for metro B2B and English-first brands. Mixed script in one line is fine on a phone.
- **Hinglish is how people talk, not a translation.** Feelings and everyday verbs in Hindi ("pareshaan", "dekh lo", "tension mat lo"); technical and business words in English ("pickup", "booking", "EMI"); connectors switch freely. Each line can lean either way.
- **Never "too pure".** Shuddh, textbook Hindi ("aapke vastron ki swachhata") reads as AI or a government notice. Say what the shopkeeper would say ("kapde ekdum saaf").
- **Write for the ear.** Short spoken lines (10–15 words), read aloud, cut anything you stumble on.
- **Cringe to avoid:** "Namaste dosto" / "Hello friends" openers, TV-news intros ("Aaj hum aapko batane wale hain…"), "yaar" in every line, emoji rain, borrowed American slang, forced rhymes, moral-lesson endings, "bro" when it isn't the brand's voice.
- **Keep as written:** brand names, prices, addresses, area names, dates.
