# Concept formats for Indian small businesses

The `format` field of a concept. Each format: what it is · best for · beats for 20–30 s · what it needs · watch out. Timings scale with `duration_sec`.

## The twelve formats

**pov**: First-person "you are here" moment; text or voice narrates the viewer's situation.
- Best for: relatable pain, teach, opinion. Low cost.
- Beats: POV situation 0–3 · the problem bites 3–8 · the fix appears 8–18 · CTA 18–25.
- Needs: one location, a phone, on-screen text.
- Watch out: needs a sharp, specific situation ("POV: mehmaan 6 baje aa rahe hain aur press wala nahi aaya"), not a vague mood.

**before-after**: The change is the story; the cut between the two is the hook.
- Best for: cleaning, salons, interiors, tailoring, repairs.
- Beats: after flash 0–2 · before 2–6 · the work 6–16 · after + offer 16–22 · CTA.
- Needs: a real job filmed both sides (plan the before shot early).
- Watch out: no exaggerated or AI-made "after"; the before must not shame anyone's body or home.

**myth-vs-fact**: Names a belief, then corrects it with a visible reason.
- Best for: teach, opinion; experts (clinics, coaching, real estate, fabric care).
- Beats: myth on screen 0–3 · "sach yeh hai" 3–6 · the proof 6–18 · what to do 18–22 · CTA.
- Needs: a real fact the owner can stand behind.
- Watch out: don't make the viewer feel stupid ("not your fault" framing helps); never debunk a named competitor.

**founder-story**: The owner, why they started or what they refuse to compromise on.
- Best for: trust, local pride, premium positioning (psychology T2, T7).
- Beats: a striking moment from the story 0–3 · the why 3–12 · today, at the shop 12–20 · CTA.
- Needs: an owner comfortable on camera, or a voice-over over real footage.
- Watch out: one turn, not a life story; no invented hardship.

**customer-story**: A real customer's situation and how it turned out.
- Best for: services with emotional stakes (wedding, home, health).
- Beats: the moment of need 0–4 · the hand-over 4–10 · the outcome 10–20 · CTA.
- Needs: a **real** customer who consents to be filmed. A staged version must not be presented as a real customer.
- Watch out: AI or actors as "real customers" is never acceptable; privacy for health and finance.

**day-in-the-life**: Follows the business (or a staff member) through a real day.
- Best for: trust, scale, showing care; hiring.
- Beats: early start 0–3 · 3–4 quick moments 3–18 · end of day + CTA.
- Needs: several short clips over one day.
- Watch out: cut to the moments that answer a customer doubt.

**process-reveal**: Shows what happens inside, step by step.
- Best for: trust-heavy services (T1): dry cleaning, kitchens, construction, clinics.
- Beats: the item arrives 0–3 · 3–4 care steps 3–16 · the result 16–21 · CTA.
- Needs: access to the real work area; the client confirms the real steps.
- Watch out: never invent steps; a messy area hurts trust (tidy, tight framing).

**comparison**: Two ways side by side (home vs shop, old vs new, A vs B option).
- Best for: helping a decision; offers with clear value.
- Beats: the two options 0–3 · side by side 3–15 · the verdict 15–20 · CTA.
- Needs: a fair, provable comparison.
- Watch out: never name or mock a competitor; compare against "the usual way" instead.

**festival-story**: A small, true festival moment where the business plays a part.
- Best for: Diwali, wedding season, Navratri, Eid, Rakhi offers (T6).
- Beats: the festival moment 0–4 · the need 4–10 · the business helps 10–18 · offer + CTA.
- Needs: the occasion's real job (cleaning, outfits, gifting, booking).
- Watch out: the diya montage cliché (psychology §4); respect for rituals; check the client's own beliefs.

**local-pride**: The city or neighbourhood as a character.
- Best for: shops rooted in an area; footfall; brand films.
- Beats: a recognisable local moment 0–3 · the business in it 3–15 · "apne sheher ka" payoff + CTA.
- Needs: real local locations (Rajwada, Sarafa, Narmada ghats, the area's main market).
- Watch out: don't put down other cities or communities; check location permissions.

**ugc-testimonial**: A real customer speaks to camera, phone-style.
- Best for: proof, trust, offers (T2).
- Beats: the customer's own line 0–3 · what they used and why 3–15 · result + CTA.
- Needs: a real customer, in their own words, with consent; disclosure if anything was given in return.
- Watch out: no scripts they wouldn't say; no AI or paid actors as customers.

**demo**: The product or service working, with the result on screen fast.
- Best for: proof, offers, anything visual.
- Beats: the result 0–2 · how 2–14 · the offer 14–20 · CTA.
- Needs: the thing working on camera.
- Watch out: a slow payoff; cut to it, then show the wait.

## Story mechanics (optional, `mechanic`)

| Mechanic | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **implied-answer** | The visual answers the question the hook asked, without saying it | "Safe hai na?" → the saree comes back in tissue |
| **social-witness** | A third person's reaction proves the point | The guest who notices the fresh curtains |
| **trojan-horse** | Looks like content until the business appears | A wedding-prep tip reel that ends at the counter |
| **reframe** | Flips the viewer's belief by the end | "Dry clean is expensive" → "cheaper than replacing the saree" |
| **contrast-without-comment** | Two realities side by side, no narration | Mother's saree then and now |

## Format by objective (quick guide)

| `objective_label` | Strong formats |
|---|---|
| leads, sales | demo, before-after, ugc-testimonial, process-reveal |
| footfall | local-pride, day-in-the-life, festival-story |
| awareness | founder-story, customer-story, pov, local-pride |
| growth | myth-vs-fact, pov, comparison |
| event | festival-story, local-pride, day-in-the-life |
