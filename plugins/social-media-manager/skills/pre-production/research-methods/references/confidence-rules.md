# Confidence and evidence rules

Insights are evidence and patterns, never guarantees. Every finding carries a **source** and a **confidence mark**. Later stages decide how much weight to give a finding from these two fields alone, so they must be honest.

## 1. The three marks

| Mark | Means | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `strong` | The same pattern from several independent sources, or from a solid sample | 3+ of 4 direct competitors do it; a theme in 8+ of 30 reviews; a formula leading the top third of 40+ reels across 6 accounts; an ad idea running 60+ days in several versions |
| `some` | A pattern in one source, or a small sample | 2–3 reviews or comments saying it; 2 outlier reels with the same hook; one rival's long-running ad |
| `weak` | A single item, or an inference | One comment; a guess from a bio; a pattern seen in only 1 reel |

Rules:
- **Sample size sets the ceiling.** Under 12 reels from an account, under 10 reviews, or under 20 comments, nothing from that source alone can be `strong`. Say the sample size in `source` ("30 read", "4 accounts, 48 reels").
- **One loud voice is not a pattern.** A single viral comment or one angry review is `weak`, however vivid.
- **Single source → never `strong`.** Corroboration from a different kind of source (reviews + comments, ads + reels) is what earns `strong`.
- **Inference is labelled.** Write "appears to", "likely" or "(inferred)" in the text when the finding is a reading, not an observation ("60+ days and 4 versions: likely working (inferred)").

## 2. Outliers and performance

- Judge reels only by the **outlier multiple**: views ÷ that account's own median of its recent reels (about 12). Above **3x** is a signal; below **1.5x** is that account's normal day. Never compare raw views across accounts.
- Don't mix accounts in one baseline, and don't compare formats on raw numbers (a reel's views vs a carousel's likes).
- Hidden counts are unknown, not zero: skip them.
- Ad counts are creative volume, never spend. Never state spend, reach, CTR or "best ad" as fact.

## 3. What may never be written

- **Invented numbers, creators, videos, ads or quotes.** Every number must come from something read in this run (or a pasted figure, marked as pasted). If a number wasn't seen, the finding has no number.
- **Predictions.** No "this will go viral", "guaranteed reach" or "will get 10x views". Write what worked for whom, with the evidence.
- **Personal identities.** No names, handles or photos of people who comment or review. Keep their words (verbatim, as written) and anonymise the source ("comments on 5 reels, 120 read"). Business and creator accounts that publish publicly may be named.
- **"None found" for a source that wasn't read.** A blocked, missing or skipped source goes in `not_read`, never into a finding as an absence.

## 4. Observation vs interpretation

Findings state the observation first, then the reading, so a reader can disagree with the reading:
"All four direct rivals post price-list carousels weekly (observed); their multiples stay under 1x, so price lists are what they overdo (interpretation)."

## 5. Small niches

If a niche is too small to see patterns (fewer than 4 relevant accounts, or a hashtag with fewer than 15 recent reels), **widen** to the category (all home services instead of laundries) or the city/state level (Indore → MP), and record it in `widened`. Findings from the wider pool are at most `some` until the client's own niche confirms them.

## 6. Freshness

- Trends and ads are dated: the source line carries the date read.
- A finding carried over from last month's sheet keeps its old date unless this run re-read the source; `monthly-diff` says which.
