# Critique pass

After the timing and fact checks, read each version as a viewer who doesn't care yet. Find where attention would drop, say why, and rewrite those beats. One round, then the humanizer.

## 1. Watch it in your head

Play the version second by second, muted first, then with sound. At each scene ask: **would I still be here?** Mark the exact scene where you'd swipe.

## 2. Name the failure (`critique_notes[].failure_mode`)

| Mode | Signs in a script | Typical fix |
|---|---|---|
| `weak-hook` | Scene 1 sets up instead of hitting; brand or logo first; the hook card isn't up at 0 s; the first spoken word comes after 0.5 s; the visual is a static face | Start on the result, the price or the stake; cut the first words; make the first frame do the work |
| `no-escalation` | Two or more middle scenes say the same thing; "aur phir" logic; the same framing repeated; no new fact after scene 3 | Open a loop and close it later; add a specific (a time, a place, a number); cut a beat; add a re-hook at ~40 % |
| `buried-payoff` | The reveal or result lands after 60 %; the offer comes before the proof that makes it worth it | Move the payoff earlier; compress the setup; tease the payoff in the hook |
| `other` | Two asks; a fact the viewer can't read in time; a line nobody would say; the CTA is vague | As the note says |

Write one note per problem: the scene, the mode, what is wrong in plain words, and the fix. "Scene 3 repeats scene 2's point; cut it and move the reveal to 8 s" helps. "Tighten the middle" doesn't.

## 3. Sweeps

Run these after naming the failures, fixing as you go:

1. **So what?** Every line answers "why should this viewer care?" A feature without its benefit gets one ("steam press" → "steam press, silk safe").
2. **Prove it.** Every claim has its proof in the scene or the next one (fact guard).
3. **Specific.** Replace an adjective with a noun or a number: "jaldi" → "kal subah tak" (if true); "achhi quality" → "har saree haath se press".
4. **One idea per scene.** Split or cut.
5. **The promise.** The hook's promise is delivered by `payoff_scene`, clearly, on screen. If the hook promised something the script can't show, change the script; never quietly change the hook.
6. **The ask.** One CTA, low effort, at the end, said and shown.

## 4. Rewrite rules

- Keep what works; rewrite only the weak beats, then re-run the timing check on the changed scenes.
- Don't trade a real detail for a smoother line.
- If a version still fails after one round, it's the structure that's wrong for this concept: switch that version to another structure (`structures.md`) rather than polish it again.

## 5. Example (fictional laundry, version A, first draft)

| Scene | Draft | Note | Fixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Logo animation, then "Shubh Laundry presents…" | `weak-hook`: brand first, the hook card arrives at 1.5 s | Curtain drawn open, "Curtain + sofa: 20% off" from frame 1 |
| 3–4 | Offer card at 8 s, before/after at 12–16 s | `buried-payoff`: the reveal lands at 80 % | Before/after moved to 5.6–10.8 s, offer after it |
| 5 | "Hum bahut achhi cleaning karte hain" | `other`: adds nothing, unprovable | Cut; the time went to the offer card |
