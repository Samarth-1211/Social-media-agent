# Retention

Short video is distributed by how long people watch. Every scene has one job: earn the next second.

## 1. The spine

**Hook → escalation → payoff → CTA**, with proof where it lands hardest.

| Beat | Job | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| `hook` | Stop the thumb, open a loop | The picked hook's three layers, from frame 1. First word in the first 0.5 s; hook line done by 3 s. No logo, greeting or "aaj hum…". |
| `escalation` | Raise the stakes or add a specific | Each one adds something new: a sharper detail, a cost, a step, a number. Join beats with **"but / so"** (lekin / isliye), never "and then" (aur phir). A beat that only repeats is cut. |
| `proof` | Make it believable | A real fact, a real shot, a real customer (fact guard). Specific beats impressive. |
| `payoff` | Deliver the hook's promise, visibly | Before 60 % of the runtime (the hooks stage asks for the halfway mark: aim there). Name it in `checks.payoff_scene`. |
| `cta` | One ask, low effort | The brief's `cta_detail`; said and shown; last 3–4 s. |

## 2. The three failure modes (diagnose every version)

1. **Weak hook: gone by 0:01.** Setup first, brand first, vague benefit, slow first line, a static face.
   **Fix:** start at the sentence you'd normally reach at second 6; put the stake or the result in frame 1; cut every word before the point.
2. **No escalation: it bleeds out.** It opens well, then the middle is the same note three times.
   **Fix:** open a loop early and close it late; reorder so each beat raises the stakes; cut a beat that adds nothing; add a mid-video re-hook ("lekin asli baat yeh hai…").
3. **Buried payoff: stalls in the middle.** The good part arrives after the viewer has left.
   **Fix:** move the payoff (or a taste of it) earlier; compress the setup; tease the payoff in the hook so they wait for it.

## 3. Devices (`retention_device`, one per scene)

| Device | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `open-loop` | A question or gap that a later scene closes. **Close every loop you open.** | "Pehle…" (before shot) → the after, 3 s later |
| `pattern-change` | A new shot, angle, place, speed or card. **One every 1.5–3 s**; a static scene never runs past 4 s. | Counter close-up → van tracking shot |
| `reveal` | The result, the after, the price, the face. | Wipe from the dull sofa to the clean one |
| `number` | A real, specific number on screen and said. | "₹299 se upar pickup free" |
| `re-hook` | A mid-video line that restarts curiosity, around 40 % on 45–60 s films. | "Aur sabse zaroori cheez abhi baaki hai." |
| `loop-back` | The last line or frame echoes the first, so a replay feels intended. | End on the same curtain, now clean |

Rules:
- **A pattern change every 1.5–3 s.** A scene may hold one shot for up to 4 s (static) or 5 s (camera moving). Within a scene, a new card, a cut-in or a sound hit also counts as a change.
- **Second-best moment near the top, best at the payoff.** Most people leave in the first seconds; give them something early.
- **Dual track:** the voiceover can say one thing while the picture shows a complementary one (VO: the price; picture: the process). Never contradict.
- **Cut dead air.** No breath at the start, no logo sting, no "toh…". The video starts on the first useful frame.
- **End clean.** The last 0.3 s is music or silence, not a trailing word. A loop-back beats a sign-off.

## 4. Pacing shape

Fast in, a medium middle with one peak (the reveal, the laugh, the number), and a quick ending. Flat energy for 20 seconds loses to a rollercoaster.

## 5. Per-length checklist

| Length | Must have |
|---|---|
| ≤ 20 s | Hook + at least 1 escalation + payoff by ~10–12 s + CTA. 5–8 scenes. |
| 21–35 s | Payoff by ~60 %; one proof beat; 7–12 scenes. |
| 36–60 s | A re-hook around 40 %; payoff by 60 %; proof after the payoff; 10–16 scenes. |
